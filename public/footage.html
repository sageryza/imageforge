Footage

Say what the clip is. Add references — a picture, a clip, a sound — and name each one in the prompt by its slot: [Image1], [Video1], [Audio1]. Tap a slot name to drop it into the box. Never describe what a reference shows; the picture is the point.

The star sends it. One tap, and the line beside it says what that tap costs. With a reference video that figure is the closest the door will say up front; the real charge lands on the card when the clip finishes.

Every clip says how long it took. drew in 2m 34s on the card is the door's own figure, not how long you were watching — close the app and it keeps drawing, and the time it reports is still the real one. A clip drawn before this says nothing rather than guessing.

Whichever door is cheapest — the line beside the star names it. Right now that is Atlas Cloud for Mini and Fast and OpenRouter for 2.0 and 2.5, but nothing here is written down: the three doors are priced for the exact clip you have set up and the cheapest one gets it. If that door refuses a reference — free, before anything draws — the job stops there and the card says why; it is never sent through another door on its own, and never drawn without its references. OpenRouter refuses any person in a reference, Atlas Cloud takes a person and refuses only a famous face.

Four models. 2.0 Mini is the default and much the cheapest. 2.0 Fast, 2.0 and 2.5 climb from there — the line beside the star says what the tap costs before you make it. Two things worth knowing: Fast is the strict one (a plain prompt with no reference at all has come back refused there with a copyright line, free and unbilled, where the same words drew on Mini), and 2.0 is the only one that does 1080p.

The last frame of a clip — the small picture under its references, for chaining one shot into the next. Tap it to see it big and save it. It rides on Atlas Cloud only, so a clip that went out through another door has none and shows no picture.

The whole prompt area folds. Tap Prompt at the top of the panel and the box, the references, the buttons and the star go away together — the clips come up to just under the header, which is the quickest way down to them. Shut, the row says the first words of what is in the box, and the project stays on it so you can still narrow what you are looking at. Nothing is lost: everything is still set the way you left it, and anything that puts words or a reference back opens it again.

Shortest first. Seconds open at the model's minimum — the length is the bill. Type the number, or tap − and +.

Sound is always on.

Divide here. The mark beside the bigger-box button cuts the box at your cursor into two blocks — a long scene becomes shot one and shot two, each its own clip. Every block has the same two buttons, and the mark in the gap between two blocks joins them back into one. With more than one block, the star sends the block you are in — the one with the gold line; tap into another to send that one instead. Nothing is sent by dividing.

Clear beside the star wipes the job — every block, the references, the marks and the seed — and leaves the model, the size, the shape and the project where you set them. It asks nothing, because undo appears next to it and puts the whole thing back, even after a reload: the cleared job is saved the moment you clear it. Tap undo again and you are back where you were. Your words are saved as you type them either way.

The seed is on every finished clip, with a button that puts it back in the box. Leave the box blank and each clip gets a fresh one. A seed repeats the opening of a short clip and then drifts — it does not survive a changed prompt or a different size, and at 15 seconds it does close to nothing.

Tiles. Each one carries two doors in the middle: ▶ plays the clip, and the list mark beside it takes you to that clip’s card — its words, its seed, its references and the button that puts them back. The first words of your own newest note on a clip sit along the top of its tile.

Projects and folders. The folder icon on the buttons row opens the projects — the same folders the character library keeps — and under each one its sub-folders (the witch commercials, say). Pick one and the header takes its name, the clips, the Recent drawer and the characters narrow to it, and every clip you send is filed there; All shows everything, with each clip saying where it belongs. New project… and, inside a project, New folder… make one; a folder is only the clips filed in it, so an emptied one goes away on its own. A scene sent from a belt page switches to its own project by itself. Every card has the same drop-down to move a clip — moving it to another project takes it out of its folder. Your words and the references already attached stay put when you switch — only what is offered narrows.

Characters. The people icon opens this film’s library. Tap a character — or one of their outfits — and their references go into the strip while the line that names them by slot goes to the top of the box. The line under each one is exactly what it will add, worked out against what you already have attached, so a second character gets [Video2] rather than the slot the library was written against. Each film is its own shelf. Taking a reference off takes its slot name out of the box and renumbers the rest, so a name never points at the wrong picture. Only the names change — the words around them are yours.

Notes. The speech mark on a clip opens a note; playing one and tapping it pauses the film and notes the second you stopped on. Either way it lands in the one place chats read their notes, and a chat answers on the note itself.

What is left on Atlas is on its own console — it has no balance to read from here.
