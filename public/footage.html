Cheapest is the default. It ranks the doors by what this exact tap costs and sends to the cheapest one that can take its shape. Pick a door yourself and it is pinned: a refusal there is the answer, and nothing is re-sent through another door on its own.

Atlas Cloud takes a real person — a photo or a video — and refuses a famous face before it draws, free. It is the only door that hands back the clip’s last frame. Cheapest for Mini and Fast on its own sale. It takes at most 9 pictures, 3 videos and 3 audios, and never audio on its own.

OpenRouter is cheapest for 2.0 and 2.5. On 2.0 it refuses any real person in a reference before it draws (free); 2.5’s gate is looser. No last frame. Credits cost 5.5% more to buy than the price you see.

APIFRAME is the dearest, 2–4x, and it is the one door that takes a first and a last frame beside other references. On 2.0 it silently drops every reference, draws a stranger and bills you — measured — so a person reference belongs on Atlas, or on 2.5.

Say what the clip is. Add references — a picture, a clip, a sound — and name each one in the prompt by its slot: [Image1], [Video1], [Audio1]. Tap a slot name to drop it into the box. Never describe what a reference shows; the picture is the point.

The star sends it. One tap, and the line beside it says what that tap costs. With a reference video that figure is the closest the door will say up front; the real charge lands on the card when the clip finishes.

Every clip says how long it took. drew in 2m 34s on the card is the door's own figure, not how long you were watching — close the app and it keeps drawing, and the time it reports is still the real one. A clip drawn before this says nothing rather than guessing.

And it says what rides on top. Whatever is in Characters & setting goes at the top of the clip, folded away or not — so under the price a line says + characters & setting on top · 40 words, and a tap on it opens the box. A whole scene left in there rides in front of the block you send.

And the line under the star says how long this one will take. usually ~1m 10s is the middle of what this exact model, size, shape and length has really taken on this door — the middle rather than the average, so one clip that sat in a queue cannot drag it. A shape nothing has drawn yet says nothing at all.

Whichever door is cheapest — the line beside the star names it. Right now that is Atlas Cloud for Mini and Fast and OpenRouter for 2.0 and 2.5, but nothing here is written down: the three doors are priced for the exact clip you have set up and the cheapest one gets it. If that door refuses a reference — free, before anything draws — the job stops there and the error says why, with the other doors that would still take it underneath it, priced. Tapping one sends that same job — your words, your references, that door. Nothing is ever sent through another door on its own, and never drawn without its references. OpenRouter refuses any person in a reference, Atlas Cloud takes a person and refuses only a famous face.

Four models. 2.0 Mini is the default and much the cheapest. 2.0 Fast, 2.0 and 2.5 climb from there — the line beside the star says what the tap costs before you make it. Two things worth knowing: Fast is the strict one (a plain prompt with no reference at all has come back refused there with a copyright line, free and unbilled, where the same words drew on Mini), and 2.0 is the only Seedance that does 1080p.

Wan 3.0 and Wan 3.0 Prime are Alibaba’s model, on Atlas Cloud only. One clip runs 2 to 30 seconds in a single pass, takes up to ten pictures, five videos and five audios, and does 480p, 720p and 1080p (no 21:9). With no reference it is text-to-video; with a first frame it is image-to-video; with references it is reference-to-video — the page picks the endpoint from what you attached. Its docs say nothing about faces: a real person in a reference went through without a word, and whether it draws a famous one is not yet measured. Prime is the dearer, stronger tier. Wan 3.0 refuses any REAL face, in a reference or in what it draws — measured. Wan 2.2 Turbo is the one that takes a real face (it runs on Atlas’s own machines, with no Alibaba filter in front of it): one picture as the first frame plus your words, five seconds, no other references and no sound, 2¢ a second at 480p. Wan 2.7 is Alibaba-hosted again (expect the face gate), takes references plus one voice, 720p and up, 10¢ a second.

MiniMax H3 is the door that takes a real person AND speaks a new line in their voice: a picture of them plus a short clip of them talking as references, and the prompt says what they say. 5 to 15 seconds, any shape, 480P or 768P (send 768P — MiniMax counts the short side, so its 480P is small), about 8¢ a second at 768P. It always draws sound. Upscale on a finished clip’s card sends it through Atlas’s upscaler to 2K as a new clip beside it, about 3¢ a second, sound and words untouched — it enlarges, it does not sharpen what the draw never had, so draw, keep what you like, then upscale the keepers.

The last frame of a clip — the small picture under its references, for chaining one shot into the next. Tap it to see it big and save it. It rides on Atlas Cloud only, so a clip that went out through another door has none and shows no picture.

Shortest first. Seconds open at the model's minimum — the length is the bill. Type the number, or tap − and +.

Sound is always on.

Characters & setting. One permanent block, two boxes, folded away until you tap it — and it sits directly above the block you are in, so what is on screen is always what that clip is about to send. Characters is that block’s own: tap into another block and its cast comes back, the way its pictures do, so shot two can be one person where shot one was the whole family. Setting is every clip’s — one room per project. Both ride at the top of the prompt, cast first. Folded, the heading says what is in both, so you can see what is riding without opening it. Leave a box empty and it adds nothing. Dividing a block gives the new half a copy of the cast; joining two puts both casts together; clear wipes the cast with the blocks (undo puts it back) and leaves the setting alone.

Divide here. The mark beside the bigger-box button cuts the box at your cursor into two blocks — a long scene becomes shot one and shot two, each its own clip. With the cursor at the end there is nothing to cut, so it makes an empty block under this one and puts you in it — a box for the next shot before there are words for it. Every block has the same buttons, and the mark in the gap between two blocks joins them back into one. With more than one block, the star sends the block you are in — the one with the gold line; tap into another to send that one instead, or tick several headings to send them appended as one clip. Nothing is sent by dividing. The join mark goes away while either of its two blocks is folded. Each block gets its own heading, and tapping the heading folds that block away — shut it shows its first words, and it still sends them if it is the one the star is pointing at. A folded block stays folded — it comes back that way after a reload, and opens by itself if anything puts words into it.

Every block keeps its own pictures. The references strip is the block you are in — tap into another one and its pictures come back, and the row above them says which block it is showing. Dividing gives both halves a copy of the strip the scene was written against, so every [Image2] still points at the same picture; joining puts the two strips together and renumbers both halves onto it. The × takes a picture off the block you are in and leaves the others alone; clear all at the end of the bar takes every picture off that block at once, with their names out of the box, and undo beside the star puts them back. Each heading says how many that block is carrying, so nothing rides on a block you are not looking at. Each heading also ends with a red sent or unsent saying whether the words in that box have gone — change one and it reads unsent again. Marking blocks sends every marked block's pictures in the one clip, with each block's names renumbered onto them — and the price beside the star is that clip's, not one block's.

Picking several blocks — any of them, in any order. Every block's heading carries a small square. Tick the ones you want and the star sends those blocks as one clip, appended in the order they sit with a blank line between — one job, one price, one refusal line. They do not have to be next to each other: block 1 and block 3 without block 2 is three taps. The star says how many it is about to send, the price beside it is that clip's, and the clip carries every ticked block's pictures with each block's names renumbered onto them. Your blocks are not touched and the ticks stay, so sending again is one tap. Tick nothing and the star is what it always was — the block with the gold line. Over $3 the first tap asks and the second sends.

Taking one block off. The × in a block’s corner takes that block off; with only one block left there is no × and clear beside the star is what empties it. Either way undo puts it back.

Clear beside the star wipes the job — every block, the references, the marks and the seed — and leaves the model, the size, the shape and the project where you set them. It asks nothing, because undo appears next to it and puts the whole thing back, even after a reload: the cleared job is saved the moment you clear it. Tap undo again and you are back where you were. Your words are saved as you type them either way.

The seed is on every finished clip, with a button that puts it back in the box. Leave the box blank and each clip gets a fresh one. A seed repeats the opening of a short clip and then drifts — it does not survive a changed prompt or a different size, and at 15 seconds it does close to nothing.

Tiles. Each one carries two doors in the middle: ▶ plays the clip, and the list mark beside it takes you to that clip’s card — its words, its seed, its references and the button that puts them back. The first words of your own newest note on a clip sit along the top of its tile.

Stitch mode. The three-boxes mark beside the ? at the top turns it on. Tap clips in the order you want them and each one wears its number; tap a numbered clip to take its number off, and the ones after it move up. A trimmed clip puts its parts in, one number each. Send to Stitch makes a stitch of them in that order and opens it — you can still move them around there, and the one button joins them. Nothing is drawn: a stitch is free.

Filters. The funnel beside the folder narrows the clips by model, resolution, when they were sent, and whether they have been trimmed; a lit funnel wears how many are on. The magnifying glass opens a search box; shutting it clears the words.

Projects and folders. The folder icon on the buttons row opens the projects — the same folders the character library keeps — and under each one its sub-folders (the witch commercials, say). Pick one and the header takes its name, the clips, the Recent drawer and the characters narrow to it, and every clip you send is filed there; All shows everything, with each clip saying where it belongs. New project… and, inside a project, New folder… make one; a folder is only the clips filed in it, so an emptied one goes away on its own. A scene sent from a belt page switches to its own project by itself. Every card has a button saying where its clip is; tapping it opens the same folders to move the clip — moving it to another project takes it out of its folder. Your words and the references already attached stay put when you switch — only what is offered narrows.

Characters. The people icon opens this film’s library. Tap a character — or one of their outfits — and their references go into the strip while the line that names them by slot goes to the top of the box. The line under each one is exactly what it will add, worked out against what you already have attached, so a second character gets [Video2] rather than the slot the library was written against. Each film is its own shelf. Taking a reference off takes its slot name out of the box and renumbers the rest, so a name never points at the wrong picture. Only the names change — the words around them are yours.

Notes. The speech mark on a clip opens a note; playing one and tapping it pauses the film and notes the second you stopped on. Either way it lands in the one place chats read their notes, and a chat answers on the note itself.

What is left, and what today cost, are the line at the top of this card — read live off Atlas Cloud's billing api every time you open it. A day's total is exact; the figure under the star is an estimate, because Atlas stamps no job id on a charge.
