# Dolores Cannon — TIME: the passage map

Source material for an illustrated piece about what the Dolores Cannon books
say about **time**. Sophie flagged it in a voice memo on 3 Aug 2026 and named
**two threads**: getting lost in time, and the joke about humans measuring
something that can't be measured.

This file is the read-everything pass. Every quote below is **verbatim from the
banked transcript**, with its book and timestamp, so narration can be cut from
the real audio later (see `docs/nde-precise-cutting.md`).

## Thread A — getting lost in time

**Three Waves of Volunteers 1 (`qzQ1P5fRPKA`), 2:32:32–2:39:04.**
One continuous scene, and the one Sophie means. Laura, regressed, is a young
man in a classroom on another world learning to travel through portals.

Verbatim, the spine of it (2:32:32–2:35:07):

> cross-legged. And it's funny, the classroom is all male and we all have
> skirts on. There are no females. There's a teacher. He's very radiant, very
> advanced. We have great respect for him.
>
> — Is he the one who taught you how to use the pendant?
>
> — I think so. I'm not really sure who. The pendant has always been there.
> It's like we're born knowing.
>
> — And one of the things you were learning was about the stars. That's
> important so that we can come and go at certain times. You have to make sure
> you know the times. There are rifts and the rifts can be dangerous to travel
> unless you travel at the right time. They were teaching us how to travel.
>
> — I'm asking my teacher. He knows. He's not getting through to me. He's too
> knowledgeable and I'm not knowledgeable enough.
>
> — He's saying there are portals and the galaxies need to align themselves a
> certain way, a certain time, and then you can just slip through the
> dimension. If I don't, I can get lost. I can go to another dimension, then
> I'll be lost and I won't have the same class or the same — I'll be out of my
> time slot. He's saying that's what a rift is. He's saying you wouldn't be
> able to get back.
>
> — Can he tell you how to avoid that?
>
> — We're supposed to learn the stars and the alignment and know when to go and
> when to get back. **He says it's like crossing a river and you have to go
> with the rapids. Otherwise, you'll get whisked away and won't come back.**
>
> — But no girls. Why no girls? Why are there no girls in our class? Too risky.
> It's too risky. There are not enough women. **They're not expendable. We
> are.**
>
> — Why do they want you to know how to time travel?
>
> — Oh, we're supposed to check on the kids to make sure they're doing okay,
> how they are growing. […] They're calling the children the ones learning. The
> humans, the learning ones.

It keeps going, and the rest is why the scene is worth more than its famous
paragraph (2:35:07–2:39:04):

- **The charts.** *"He plots it on charts. He keeps charts, many, many charts,
  and he plots the information. There are more than just me that go out."*
- **Travelling without a body.** *"I don't feel physical when I travel. I feel
  all over the place. I can see. Scary."*
- **Graduation.** *"We're all standing in a hall, and each one he touched our
  forehead and told us that we had a third eye, and that we now needed to go
  out."*
- **After.** He wanders, teaching shepherds: *"They seem so grounded. They think
  they have to stay there with their sheep. They don't have to. They can go
  anywhere. They don't believe they can do it."*
- **His death.** *"I'm in the meadow, a field, and I'm attacked by a huge cat,
  but I've lived a long life. I'm still alone and not upset with this cat. It
  was fine. I'm at peace."*
- **What he learned.** *"I learned just to believe. We can do anything that we
  put our minds to. Nothing is impossible."*

Counted honestly this is **~16 beats**, not six. The beat the passage turns on
is the river — it is the teacher's own metaphor and the only line that explains
*why* timing is the whole skill.

## Thread B — measuring the unmeasurable

Five candidates. They differ mainly in whether anyone is *in* them.

### B1 — the line itself (best words, no scene)
**Custodians 1 (`mMLQaQ7jsts`), 0:42:29–0:45:10.** Dolores in her own voice,
opening Chapter 2, "Condensed or distorted time":

> We are hampered by being trapped within our concept of linear time. **It has
> been said we may be the only planet in the universe that has invented a way to
> measure something that does not exist.** I have been told many times in my
> work that time is only an illusion, an invention of man. The aliens do not
> have this concept and they have told me that man will never travel in space
> until he overcomes the erroneous idea of time. […]
>
> If we lived on a planet that revolved around its sun in a different manner,
> how would we measure time? Suppose there was always sunlight or always
> darkness. Suppose the planet had two suns. Would they measure time in a
> different way? Or would they decide there was no need for such an
> inconvenience? What about beings traveling on spaceships for long periods of
> time, hurtling through space with no reference points to distinguish day and
> night and no reason to mark off the seasons and years. It is no wonder they do
> not understand our purpose for time and often can make no sense of it.

This is essay, not session — no person, no consequence. But it is where the joke
actually lives: *"the only planet in the universe that has invented a way to
measure something that does not exist"* is built like a joke. It is also full of
concrete pictures (two suns, endless daylight, a ship with no day or night).

### B2 — the same idea as a scene (best scene)
**Keepers of the Garden 1 (`VXO-C9w3TDw`), 2:30:27–2:32:46.** Dolores is
questioning a being who has just told her its planet's name can't be said:

> — I should have known it would be the same problem with answering questions
> about time, **but habit prevailed.** Do you have any way of telling time where
> you are? Such as do you have years as we know them?
>
> — No, we don't have time because we are on the fourth dimension where there is
> no time. **There would be no need to measure something which does not apply.**

The human in this scene is **Dolores**, and the consequence is that she cannot
stop asking. She says so herself: *"I am always trying to pinpoint where we are
with names, dates, and places."*

### B3 — the wry beat
**Keepers of the Garden 2 (`rvfOUg4zVc0`), 6:40:32.** Dolores catching herself
doing it again, and the being obliging her anyway:

> — **You keep saying there's no such thing as time, but I was wondering when
> that occurred.**
>
> — We would translate in your terms roughly several million of your years.
> Although the analogy is somewhat rough.

### B4 — "your imaginary time"
**Three Waves 1 (`qzQ1P5fRPKA`), 5:42:10–5:43:03.** Jackie, who remembers being
a block of blue light:

> — When I was with them, it was always complete when I was with them. **Time
> doesn't exist.** When I was a block, a solid block of blue light. That was my
> happiest time.
>
> — Was that when you were younger?
>
> — **If we were to talk of your imaginary time in years, it would probably be
> 500,000 years ago.** I was happy then. A comfortable solid blue light.

Note what Dolores's question does to it — she asks "were you younger?" of a
being describing a state with no time in it, and gets corrected gently.

### B5 — the unit nobody can explain
**Keepers 1 (`VXO-C9w3TDw`), 0:26:18.** Phil, describing a colony:

> — Do you know how long they have been on this planet?
>
> — **Time doesn't correlate to Earth years, but it has been seven chronometers.
> Although I can't explain what chronometers are.**

## The "laugh" was never a laugh — it is B1 (settled 8 Aug 2026)

**Do not repeat this hunt.** The memo's *"they think it's funny haha"* was read
here as a recorded laugh, and a long search was run for one: every `[laughter]` /
`laughed` / `laughing` marker (19), every `amuse*` / `chuckl*` / `silly` (14),
plus `you humans`, `curious to us`, `in a hurry`, `why do you worry`, `strange
that you`. Every hit read. **No laugh about time exists in these seven books.**

Sophie then clarified: she never meant a literal laugh, only *"isn't it strange
or interesting or funny that humans measure something that doesn't exist"* — and
suggested searching the words **measure** and **doesn't exist**. That search
(`measur[a-z]*[^.]{0,80}exist` either order) returns exactly **two hits, both in
Custodians 1**:

- 0:43:12 — *"…invented a way to **measure something that does not exist**"* (B1)
- 0:46:21 — *"If they can't see it, **measure it**, touch it, or dissect it, **it
  doesn't exist**"* (the nuts-and-bolts investigators, same essay)

So the passage is **B1**, and the "funny" is the shape of the line, not an event
in the room. Her wording is the pointer to a *phrase*, not to a *scene* — worth
remembering the next time a memo describes a tone.

The two passages below are **not** it. They are filed only as evidence that these
beings are on record finding human habits funny, about other subjects:

**Custodians 2 (`L0TSZDqQlnU`), 11:23:15–11:24:32.** Dolores stops to change the
tape in her recorder:

> — I must interrupt you a moment. I have to tend to my little black box. You
> understand, don't you? […] We don't have the ability to remember everything
> that is said. We need a machine to capture the words.
>
> — Oh yes, and that's what I was going to say that we have rather **a patient
> tolerance** for all of you on earth who are so sincerely interested as you are
> and want to record and remember and be able to replay. And so it would be
> almost like you as an accomplished adult human looking at a child and
> realizing they might have to **add numbers by counting on their fingers**. You
> are like that to us, but we do not mean that condescendingly. It's almost like
> a **loving amusement** slightly and a tolerance and acceptance. It's perfectly
> all right.

This is the closest thing in the books to the tone Sophie remembers — and it is
even about *counting* — but the subject is **memory, not time**.

**Keepers 2 (`rvfOUg4zVc0`), 1:33:44.** After Dolores laughs at their picture of
God:

> — We are learning patience with you as much as you are learning patience with
> us. […] We wish you to understand that we enjoy these sessions, for **we
> derive much humor from these insights into human nature.** […] We thank you
> and wish to come again, for we enjoy this as much, if not more than you, for
> **there are 12 of us here who are laughing with you.**

Then they sober immediately when she asks if they're playing games: *"We are on
a mission which has no humor to it."*

Do not build a beat around a laugh that was never recorded.

## Listening copies (cut 8 Aug 2026)

Reading ten passages is a lot of work, so every candidate is cut from the real
audiobook audio and banked at Storage `dolores-time/clips/v1/<ID>.mp3`
(deckfactory, public, immutable-cached). 19.4 minutes across ten clips.

`A` 2:28 · `A2` 4:09 · `B1` 2:41 · `B2` 2:14 · `B3` 0:28 · `B4` 0:56 ·
`B5` 0:31 · `LAUGH` 1:07 · `LAUGH2` 2:36 · `JANET` 2:12

**How they were cut, and why it cost nothing.** Cut points come from the
transcript's own `segments` timings, snapped to segment boundaries and then
anchored to the sentence the passage actually opens on — several naive cuts
started mid-sentence ("cross-legged.", "she arrived home"). ffmpeg with 12ms edge
fades, mono 128k mp3, **no loudnorm**. Source audio downloaded whole first
(347MB–604MB each; ffmpeg HTTP seek does not work from a chat sandbox).

**Segment timings are accurate enough for listening cuts — whisper is not needed
for this step.** Word-level alignment is only required for a finished narration
where a ragged word matters (`docs/nde-precise-cutting.md`). Total spend here was
about **2¢**: a 14-second head of each clip transcribed with whisper-1 purely to
*verify* the cut landed on the intended line, which caught one bad head (B4
opened on "to do what I need to do") and nothing else.

Verifying openings this way is cheap and worth doing every time — it is the only
check that proves a cut list matches the audio rather than the transcript.

## Not one of her two threads, but the best time-scene in the books

**Custodians 1 (`mMLQaQ7jsts`), 1:03:10.** Janet, a businesswoman who wanted
nothing to do with any of this, drove 50 miles home from Little Rock **in 15
minutes** on a four-lane interstate that was completely empty at midnight. Her
dogs went berserk when she arrived. She was embarrassed to talk about it and was
certain there was a logical explanation. *"She said there were at least 30 other
things she would rather have been doing that weekend."*

Person, consequence, picture, and the whole thing is about time. Flagged as an
option only — Sophie did not name it.

## The trap, for whoever draws this

Time is abstract, and the stock images for it — a clock, an hourglass, a spiral
of numbers — are exactly what to avoid. The drawable moments here are the ones
with a person and a consequence in them: a boy aiming himself at a gap between
galaxies; a teacher with many, many charts of where everyone went; a woman with
a notebook asking a being from the fourth dimension what year it is; a
businesswoman standing in her own driveway at midnight while her dogs scream.

Where a passage has no scene (B1), say so rather than illustrating the concept.

## Sophie's picks and the working cut (8 Aug 2026)

Chosen: **B1** (opening) → **A** (classroom). **B4** cut but not placed. Out:
B2, B3, B5, LAUGH2, Janet. LAUGH held as "iffy… we'll have to see the shape".

**She wants ONLY the sentences that appeared in the review artifact — the text
around the "…" is not to be reinstated.** The first cut restored those elided
words (it read them as the natural continuous run) and she rejected it: *"You
added all sorts of other words."* So the working cut is a **word-level splice**
of exactly the quoted lines, verified by transcribing the finished audio back and
diffing against the artifact text. This shortens the piece from ~2:57 to **~46s**
and makes it deliberately elliptical — that is the intent, not an accident.

Her edits, applied: B1 drops its first sentence and opens on *"It has been
said…"*; A drops the whole classroom introduction (skirts / the teacher) and
opens on *"There are rifts…"*; B4 opens on the SECOND thing the subject says,
deletes Dolores's *"Was that when you were younger?"*, and moves the first thing
he says to the end.

**Assets.** Sixteen sentence-level mp3s at Storage
`dolores-time/sentences/v3/<key>.mp3`, assembled passages and spine at
`dolores-time/clips/v3/`. Sentence boundaries, speakers and timings are in the
session's `sentences.json`; each was located by local alignment (Smith-Waterman
over tokens) against whisper word timestamps, then hand-checked — the aligner
reliably drops a leading word ("There's", "He's"), so **always verify the head of
every fragment** rather than trusting the match score.

**Arrangement is Sophie's, live.** The reorder page writes to
`GET /api/chatfeed/verdict?chat=dolores-cannon-time&sheet=time-passages-v1`, one
item per passage (`order-B1`, `order-A`, `order-B4`), value = comma-separated
sentence keys with a `-` prefix meaning deleted. **Read that before re-cutting** —
it, not this file, is the current running order.
