## Why it is this scene

Her own note on why the drawn film is funnier than the script: the model
invented the height dialogue, so the film's running joke is the three
quarters / six eighths that are the same number. Her words the same day,
looking at the curtain: *"and the taller changes the story a little."*

A retaliation scene is jonathan answering that — and it has to be **physical,
not spoken**, because the height joke arrived as dialogue the model made up and
the payoff should be something you can see. It also obeys the one thing this
film has taught us about mini (see `refs.json` `herLines`): **an event needs a
cause.** The books in the shoes are the cause; the height is the effect.

Where it goes, if she wants it: after **Weeks pass** (`sj-3`) and before
**The white paint** (`sj-4`). It is the last beat of the war, and the paint
scene is where the war ends.

## The scene

> jonathan waits until he hears the shower. then he gets the tape measure out
> and marks a pencil line on the doorframe, standing as straight as he can,
> chin up, on his tiptoes. he looks at the line. he is not satisfied.
>
> he goes to the closet and comes back with two thick books, and slides them
> into his shoes. he stands against the doorframe again, wobbling, and draws a
> second line above the first. he steps back and admires it. he crosses his
> arms and waits.
>
> sean comes round the corner mid-sentence, not looking at him, wearing
> enormous platform boots he has found somewhere, talking about something else
> entirely. he is a foot taller. he walks straight past.
>
> jonathan looks at the line. he looks at sean. the books slide out of his
> shoes.
