# House Voice — The Sophie Experiment (punchy pass, July 2026)

Sophie read her original (lightly-stitched, verbatim-her) drafts against a
punched-up "smart blog / personal-essay" rewrite of the Jon date and preferred
the punchy one for the whole book. This is the locked voice spec so every date
matches. She will edit each date herself afterward to reclaim it as hers — so
leave her room, don't over-finish.

## The voice
First-person, wry, fast, self-aware, a little merciless — about herself as much
as the men. A sharp personal-essay register: her stories told with momentum and
jokes, while the events, feelings, and weird specifics stay 100% hers.

## The one rule above all: CONSISTENCY
Same narrator, same wit level, on every single date. The punch lands harder or
softer depending on the material, but the *register never changes* — no date is
solemn, none is a stand-up set. If you read any two dates back to back they
should sound like one person wrote them in one sitting.

## Rules
1. **Her words are still the source.** Pull her exact phrases from the raw
   journal and keep them. Punch the connective tissue *around* her lines; don't
   overwrite the lines themselves.
2. **Keep the murk.** Her strangest, most specific, unexplained, or
   contradictory lines stay VERBATIM and unexplained — "I don't know why I
   needed to"; the debit card she found funny; a feeling that's two things at
   once. Never resolve or explain them. This is the stuff no one but her could
   have written; it's the reason the book is hers.
3. **Punch = sharpen, not invent.** Pick the sharper of two things she already
   said and give it room. Add rhythm: short sentences, fragments, an occasional
   direct address ("Reader,"). Land the joke she implied. NEVER invent events or
   feelings she didn't have.
4. **Techniques, used sparingly** (so they still hit): a hook first line;
   section-y momentum; deadpan asides; a punchy button to end on.
5. **Length:** roughly match the current curated draft. Don't pad the logistics
   we already cut back in (outfit inventories, navigation, which books he
   returned, repeated beats).
6. **Structure:** 1–3 pages, split at natural breaks. `dek` = a short, wry
   italic subtitle (can be punchier than the old ones).

## Do NOT
- Turn it into a listicle or number the "reasons."
- Add pop-culture references she wouldn't make.
- Explain her psychology or moralize.
- Close every line — a slightly open ending beats a tidy one.
- Break consistency for a cheap laugh on one date.

## Calibration exemplar — Jon, punched up
> Jon Storch broke the streak — mostly because I mentioned I was going to an art
> museum and he decided that counted as an invitation. … He wanted neither and
> went a little pale — I'm fairly sure he thought I was fattening him up for the
> check. … Here's the thing I never told him: I watched that woman resign. In
> person. At my brother's graduation. From Brown. … Then I paid the check with my
> debit card, which, for reasons I still can't defend, I found hilarious. … And I
> ran a full internal debate: do I signal I'm on a date, or talk to this man
> *more*, so my actual date understands I'm not especially invested? Reader, I
> kept talking to the armor guy. … I realized, right there on the steps, that I
> might be better off alone. At least alone I still get to have my own thoughts.

Note how the jokes ride on top but every event and feeling is hers, the weird
lines ("I don't know why I needed to," the debit card) stay untouched, and the
logistics are gone.
