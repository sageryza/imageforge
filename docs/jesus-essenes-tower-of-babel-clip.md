# "Jesus and the Essenes" — Tower of Babel / lost-telepathy clip

Source material for a video clip. Sophie has her own copy (listening via Spotify);
the actual audiobook narration was NOT sourced or downloaded here — DRM-protected,
paid content, and ripping it isn't something to do regardless of clip length. The
plan instead: TTS-narrate the quoted text ourselves.

## Where it is
- **Book:** *Jesus and the Essenes*, Dolores Cannon (Ozark Mountain Publishing,
  paperback ~290pp).
- **Chapter:** 16, "Creation, Catastrophe and the Kaloo."
- **Spotify audiobook timestamp:** Chapter 16, **20:29** (chapter runs 45:20 total,
  confirmed live against Sophie's own playback 2026-07-23).
- **Paperback page (approx, from a scanned copy, not verified against Sophie's
  own print copy):** ~177–179.

## How the chapter gets there
Suddi (the regressed Essene) and D (the researcher/Dolores) are discussing an
earlier catastrophe and "the Kaloo," and the fragmentary nature of Essene oral
history — then D pivots to ask about Noah specifically, which leads into the
flood, which leads into Suddi volunteering the telepathy tangent.

## Full passage (verbatim from a scanned copy — spot-check against Sophie's own
copy before final use; wording is consistent across multiple independent pulls
but not certified word-for-word)

> **S:** "It is said that the very earth exploded, as if to spew man off its surface."
>
> **D:** "I wondered if this catastrophe was the same one Suddi had mentioned before that was connected with the wandering of the Kaloo."
>
> **S:** "I do not know. You see, our knowledge comes down to us in bits and tatters and pieces and we must piece this together here and say, 'Well, what is this?' And so not all threads are whole. And this is what we are trying to do. We are trying to make everything whole and put it together."
>
> **D:** "Put all the pieces together and see if you get the whole story. That's why I'm interested. You know when books are changed from one language to another, many things are added and taken away."
>
> **D:** "The biblical account of Noah and his ark has always been a favourite of mine, and I was curious to see what he would say about it."
>
> **S:** "Yes, about the flood that came? 'Twas strong of hope, this may be why it is a favourite."
>
> **D:** "Why would Yahweh cause a great flood?"
>
> **S:** "Again I believe that it were more a case of continually fighting against the earth."
>
> **S:** "It is said that he was out there for approximately two turns of the moon. Sixty days . . . no, fifty-eight."
>
> **S:** "I remember about why the flood came about, though. This was part of the time when that man was struck, so that he no longer was able to speak one-to-one to everyone."
>
> **D:** "Before this ability was lost, could they communicate with people over long distances?"
>
> **S:** "Yes, it was as if they were with you. It was taken away because man was proud."
>
> **S:** "This knowledge was lost and it was closed up. And therefore there was great confusion upon the world. They spoke to one as one, to think was for the other to know. And this was lost, this ability, because of their doing."
>
> **S:** "They thought, they told each other, 'If we do this great thing, we can become as great as Yahweh and find the way to be even greater, to have more power.' And because of this, it had been lost and the confusion was brought."
>
> **S:** "Yahweh took away this ability and man was struck dumb because he had never had to communicate with others in any other way, and it was a great loss. Then he learned to speak with the mouth with words. Before this, there was no need."
>
> **Narrator:** "This story sounded familiar. Maybe this was where the story of the Tower of Babel came from and what it is supposed to signify. The loss of telepathic powers through misuse."

`D` = the researcher (Dolores), `S` = Suddi, the regressed Essene.

## Copyright approach
No safe-harbor clip length exists (fair use is a four-factor balancing test, not
a duration rule). Using the actual audiobook narration leans harder on the
"effect on the market" factor than quoting the text for commentary does. Decision:
narrate the quoted passage ourselves via TTS rather than clip the paid narration.
