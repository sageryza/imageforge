# Flash-Lessons — the core intent (Sophie's vision)

This is the through-line for the illustrated tap-through lessons, the Story Room
"summary" idea, the corner-icon map, and the nodes-and-boxes grammar. It was
worked out in a long conversation that later got compacted; this file exists so
the intent is never lost again. Written from Sophie's own words — keep it that
way. If a future chat is missing early context, read this first.

## The product (what we're actually building)
Short **TikToks / Reels that teach elusive concepts fast** — things people never
managed to learn, or never cared about. The trick is sneaky: they think they're
watching a fun little video, but they're actually learning something hard. Get
them invested and excited about something no one told them to care about, and the
lesson lands on its own. Format = **Imprint-style flash-cards** (full-screen,
tap-through, one idea per card) that become narrated videos. The reference points
are the **Imprint** app and **The Matrix** — that flash of images when Neo
learns; our brains can learn that way and we're not taking advantage of it.

## The one rule that governs every lesson: concept is the spine, metaphors are offshoots
- **Make the CENTER the abstract concept** — not a single metaphor. Not "Mars =
  aggression." Keep the abstract concept abstract, and be explicit that
  **different metaphors cover different parts of the mechanism** — little
  offshoots — but we always come back to the spine, which is the abstraction.
- **Never lean on one central metaphor.** Sophie explicitly rejected the single
  "mixing board" metaphor. Realistically nothing is exactly like one other thing
  (or it would just be that thing), so a lesson is the abstract concept plus a
  handful of small per-part metaphors, each flagged and then set aside.
- This is why the corner-icon **map** exists: it calls attention *when we switch
  which metaphor/diagram we're using*, so the viewer keeps the spine in view.

## Nodes & boxes — the grammar of relationships
A thought/story/idea is **nodes with connectors**. A node is a thing; you assert
a relationship by **drawing a box around some nodes** — the box *is* the claim
"these belong together." Sophie's key insight (`nd-boxes`): *the data stays the
same; the boxes we draw around it change* — meaning lives in the frame, not the
dots. Same engine as "where do you crop art" and the Tupperware (which container
fits). The SHAPE of the box encodes the KIND of relationship: tight=same,
loose=cluster, overlapping=Venn, nested=subset, dashed=tentative, colored=lens.
**Two kinds of set** (her SET-card note): *sameness* boxes (share a trait) vs
*fit* boxes (complete each other, like a valid SET — complementarity, not
sameness). Roots: the memory-library "Xi" idea — a cluster of similar
experiences all connecting to one memory, equally connected but some more
important / with other connections.

## Summary = the shape of a story
The Story Room "summary" is the **shape of the video**: the few key beats with
arrows between them, so you remember what a story is about at a glance (Moon
Milk = milk from the moon → the kitchen talk → releasing the Epstein files;
everything else is connective tissue). The deeper idea Sophie wants explored:
**the brain encodes a story into a shape**, and a *different* story takes a
*different* shape — and some parts live only in your head, never in the telling.
So "make a shape for a story" means find that story's structural form (linear /
loop / spiral / funnel / branch), not an arbitrary glyph.

## The map (corner icons)
An intro/meta lesson frames the whole method: someone walking a path reading a
fold-out map (first-person POV). As a lesson runs, small **metaphor** and
**diagram** icons (~1 inch, top-right) slide in when the concept switches, so
each is a little wayfinding marker. The **tricycle** is the marker for the
set-theory lesson — "this is the training-wheels version of what you're doing
now; it isn't the end goal" (orange frame, yellow wheels, pink seat, streamers).

## Entry point + audience
**Astrology first** — Sophie needs it for the Secretly a Witch business and finds
it genuinely hard, so it's the proof case (start: splitting the sky into twelve
chunks). Then the method generalizes to anything hard.

## How Sophie learns (design for this)
"Very smart but not good at facts; doesn't like applying her brain unless she has
to. If there's a simpler way, she'd rather BUILD it than learn it the hard way,
even if that takes twenty times as long." Lessons should make the hard thing
click, not drill it.

## The Compare tab should look like Imprint
The lesson index (Compare tab) should be a **grid of icon tiles** grouped by
theme (like Imprint's Explore Courses), and where lessons lead into each other,
show the **connected path** ("the maze"). Prototype = a hub page; the real
version may be a change to the app's Compare tab itself.

## Voice (already enforced elsewhere, restated here)
Aspirational, not consoling. No AI-tells. See `docs/witch-school-lessons.md` for
the full list. Public copy must not read as AI-written.
