## 1. The research, in one paragraph

The popular version ("Christmas is Saturnalia with a coat of paint") is mostly
wrong on the specifics — historians have pulled the Saturnalia, Sol Invictus,
Santa-is-Odin and mistletoe-druid claims apart. But the honest version is a
BETTER story for a commercial: Christmas has been rebuilt so many times that
almost nothing in it is original to anything. A Norse king moved a pagan feast
onto it by law and made ale brewing mandatory. A pope told missionaries to keep
the pagan temples and the animal feasts. The Puritans banned it for being a
drunken pagan riot. And nearly everything that *feels* ancient — the tree in the
house, the kiss under mistletoe, reindeer, the red suit — was invented between
1780 and 1931 by poets, a queen, a cartoonist and a soft-drink company.

**So the commercial's true claim is not "secretly pagan." It is "secretly a
remix," and the pagan part is the oldest layer, not the whole thing.** The
scripts below lean on the solid facts and leave the debunked ones out, so the
spot can be funny without a historian in the comments.

## 2. Fact sheet — three tiers

### SAFE TO SAY (documented, dated)

- **Yule is a real pagan midwinter feast and the word survived.** Bede (c. 725)
  says the pagan Anglo-Saxons called December and January *Giuli* (Yule) and
  began their year on Dec 25, "the day on which we now celebrate the birth of
  the Lord," with a night they called *Modranecht* — Mothers' Night.
- **A king legislated Yule onto Christmas.** *Heimskringla* (Hákon the Good's
  saga, reign c. 934–961): Hákon "made a law that the festival of Yule should
  begin at the same time as Christian people held it, and that every man,
  under penalty, should brew a meal of malt into ale, and therewith keep the
  Yule holy as long as it lasted." Before him Yule began on "midwinter night"
  (roughly mid-January by most readings) and ran three days.
- **A pope said: keep the temples, keep the feasts.** Gregory the Great to
  Mellitus, AD 601 (preserved in Bede): don't destroy the pagan temples,
  sprinkle them with holy water, put relics in, and since the people are used
  to slaughtering and feasting on oxen to their gods, let them keep doing it
  on the saints' days — "so that while some gratifications are outwardly
  permitted them, they may the more easily consent to the inward
  consolations."
- **Medieval Christmas was misrule.** Twelve days of feasting, heavy drinking,
  a "Lord of Misrule" (often the poorest man in the parish, waited on by the
  rich), and wassailing — the poor going door to door demanding drink and food
  from the rich, sometimes violently. Ronald Hutton (the leading historian of
  British seasonal custom) traces wassailing and the crop/tree blessings to
  pre-Christian practice.
- **The Puritans banned it.** England under Cromwell (1647), then Massachusetts
  Bay Colony 1659–1681: a five-shilling fine for anyone "observing any such day
  as Christmas or the like, either by forbearing of labor, feasting, or any
  other way." Their stated reason: no scripture for it, and it was pagan
  revelry.
- **The Victorians built the one we have.**
  - 1823 — Clement Clarke Moore's *A Visit from St. Nicholas* invents the
    sleigh, the EIGHT REINDEER and their names, and the chimney routine.
  - 1843 — Dickens's *A Christmas Carol*; the family-and-charity Christmas.
  - 1848 — the *Illustrated London News* prints Victoria, Albert and the
    children round a table-top tree; Britain and then America copy it.
  - 1863 onward — Thomas Nast's cartoons fix Santa's look: the beard, the
    belly, the North Pole.
  - 1931 — Haddon Sundblom's Coca-Cola paintings settle the red suit, black
    boots and wide belt as THE Santa.
- **The kiss under the mistletoe is from the 1780s.** A 1719 apothecary treatise
  on mistletoe mentions no kissing; the first record is George Colman's 1784
  comic opera *Two to One*. Pliny's druids and their golden sickles are real,
  but nobody connects them to midwinter or to kissing.
- **The indoor Christmas tree is medieval German and Christian**, not pagan:
  1300s Rhineland forestry rules limiting fir-cutting at Christmas, decorated
  trees in Baltic town squares by the 1500s, the "Paradise tree" of the
  medieval Adam-and-Eve plays. Evergreens in midwinter are a general human
  habit, not a specific pagan rite that got stolen.

### SAY CAREFULLY (true-ish, hedge the wording)

- **"December 25 was picked to cover a pagan sun festival."** The Sol Invictus
  feast on Dec 25 is first attested in 354 AD; Christian writers were already
  naming Dec 25 by c. 235 (Hippolytus), and the likeliest reason is arithmetic
  (conception at the spring equinox, March 25, plus nine months). Some
  scholars think the Romans put THEIR feast on the Christian date. Wording
  that survives: "the date landed on the darkest week of the year, right where
  every culture already had a party."
- **"Santa is Odin."** Odin has a beard and one skaldic poem calls him *Jólnir*
  (the Yule one). That is the whole case. No gifts, no red, no chimneys, no
  reindeer (Moore, 1823). Usable only as a joke ABOUT the claim.
- **"The yule log is pagan."** Origin genuinely unknown — medieval, Roman,
  Germanic, Slavic and Albanian candidates. Say "nobody knows where the yule
  log came from," which is funnier anyway.

### DON'T SAY (debunked)

- Christmas is Saturnalia. Saturnalia was Dec 17, stretched at most to Dec 23,
  never the 25th. **(THE ENDING in `SCRIPTS.md` says this out loud — answering
  the claim is not the same as making it, and it is the strongest twelve seconds
  in the folder.)**
- Mistletoe kissing is a druid rite.
- The tree is a pagan idol the church stole.
- Santa's reindeer are Odin's eight-legged horse.
