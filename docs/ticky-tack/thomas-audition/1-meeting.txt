Thomas is a boy with matted hair, a slender frame, gripping two bags, one canvas and one leather, both black. So pale he looks almost white; his size due not to his age but to lack of sustenance. Disheveled hair, dark smudges on his face, bags under his eyes, brown eyes. He speaks in a thin, tired drawl, slightly British. Wide eyes, always a little anxious, his brow often furrowed, mouth bent into a thin twist. A waffle weave shirt and black pants two sizes too large for him. The girl has gray eyes and matted, disheveled hair, and wears a flowered dress over striped leggings and mismatched socks.

Night at the edge of a freeway, beside a hedge, no lights anywhere. Thomas sits on the ground with his two bags. The girl walks up and stops a foot from him. Camera at eye level. Thomas, his voice utterly flat and too polite: "Can I use your phone?" The girl: "No." Thomas stands up suddenly, his dirt-streaked face alight with anger: "Not one goddamn person, can you believe that? Not one. Goddamn. Person." He looks directly at her but carries on as if he were speaking to someone else, and steps backwards into the hedge, dragging his luggage with him. Then, with something of a snarl: "I've been thinking of ending things."
