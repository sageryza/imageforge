# Weird Games to Play with Friends

Sophie's book: one weird game per spread, 1–3 pages each. Each game gets ONE
large illustration; the rest of the page is writing (the incident it came from,
the rules as they actually worked out, who lost).

## The look
- **Style: sage sandy mirror** — her scanned ink-and-watercolour page
  (`refs/sage-sandy-mirror.png`), attached as a pure style reference through
  gpt-image-2 edits. **Write no style description** — the reference does the
  work (see `docs/nde-watercolor.md`; same rule).
- **Aspect ratios vary per game** and images do NOT fill the page. A wide
  horizontal for a doorway, a tall vertical for a stairwell or a diner booth, a
  small square for a close crop of a face. The page is mostly text.
- Night, lamplight and single flames run through the whole book — most of these
  games happen after midnight.

## The cover
An **invented** game, not one of the real ones, carrying pieces of several.

**The Chain.** Everyone in a dark house has to keep holding hands while
carrying on doing whatever they were doing.

Cover image: a cutaway of a house at night — every room lit by one small flame,
someone doing something absurd in each (one halfway out a window, one asleep
under a table, two on the stairs out of breath), and a chain of held hands
running through the whole house, up the stairs and out the front door into the
dark. Hand-lettered title on the door or on a bedsheet banner.

# The real games (Sophie's)

### 1. Meet in the Middle
Zach and Doug's house, dark, with Alex McBroom there for no clear reason.
Everyone takes a candle, everyone starts in a different part of the house, and
everyone goes — and of course you all end up meeting in the middle. They met on
the stairs. There was a prize, and there was a forfeit: Doug got a hand job.

**Image:** all of them meeting on the stairs, out of breath, each holding a
single tea light. Everything around them dark except the stairs. Tall vertical.

### 2. Loud Elevator
At the lama (the Lama Foundation?). You are as loud as possible between floors —
stamping, shouting — and the instant the doors open and someone gets in, total
silence. Then the doors close and it starts again.

**Image:** the four of them mid-stamp inside the elevator, doors halfway open on
a corridor. Mid-motion, everything blurred except the doors. Square-ish.

### 3. Charlie's game (working title: **Full Bladder**)
Charlie invented it at a beer pong night: how close can you get to peeing
without peeing. Night, on a tiled balcony, beer pong going on behind — most
people playing, everyone holding a red Solo cup and drinking more beer. The
comedy is that everyone stood there checking themselves and checking each other
to judge how badly the others had to go, which of course tells you nothing.

**Image:** the balcony at night — the beer pong table behind, a loose circle in
front holding red Solo cups, everyone glancing down and sideways at each other
with total seriousness. Wide horizontal. (v1 drew a hallway outside a bathroom;
superseded, kept in the Assets tab as history.)

### 4. Nice to Meet You
With Michael, at a meetup group or a pool. You meet the same people over and
over, shaking hands each time: "Hey, how are you? Nice to meet you." If they say
"how are you", you say "good, how are you", and they say "good, how are you",
and it goes as long as they'll hold it.

**Image:** a loop of handshakes — the same two hands drawn four or five times
around a circle, the people getting slightly more strained each time round.

### 5. The Specific Item
Played alone. Go into a supermarket and ask for a very specific product that
does not exist. Score = how many employees end up looking for it. Record: three.

**Image:** an aisle from above, three employees at three different points in it,
all searching, and her standing calm in the middle. Wide horizontal.

### 6. Drowning
The pool, with Zach, Niha and Zach's girlfriend. Niha called for help — everyone
swam over, "oh my god Niha, are you okay, what's happening" — and she was fine.
She hadn't been drowning. Then everyone took a turn: you pretend to drown, and
everyone comes and makes sure you're okay.

**Image:** from underwater looking up — one figure thrashing at the surface,
three sets of legs converging on her, sun through the water. Tall vertical.

# The invented games (to fill the rest of the book)

### The Long Goodbye
Everyone puts their coat on at the front door and the game is that you cannot be
the one who leaves. The conversation restarts as many times as it takes. Loser
is whoever actually opens the door.

**Image:** the lit doorway seen from out in the dark street, four people wedged
into it in coats, one foot already on the step, everyone mid-sentence. Wide
horizontal.

### One Word Each
The group can only speak one word at a time, in rotation — including to
strangers. Best played somewhere you have to order food. A round of drinks takes
ten minutes and the waiter never says anything about it.

**Image:** a diner booth from the waiter's side of the table, four faces looking
up, each mouth open on a different syllable, the notepad still blank. Vertical.

### The Understudy
Pick someone at the party. Copy their posture and gestures exactly, half a
second behind, all night, saying nothing about it. You win by lasting until they
catch you.

**Image:** two people on a couch in the identical slouch, one a beat behind —
same pose, drink slightly lower. Small horizontal.

### Search Party
You and one friend split up in a bar or a park. Each of you asks strangers if
they've seen the other, describing them badly. You win when a stranger actually
joins the search.

**Image:** a night park, three strangers pointing in three different directions,
and the friend they're looking for visible in the background, unnoticed. Wide
horizontal.

### Whisper Fight
A screaming argument performed at whisper volume in a crowded place — all the
hands, all the faces, none of the sound. The round ends when someone asks if
you're okay.

**Image:** two people nose to nose over a bar table, every vein and gesture of a
full-blown fight, the bartender behind them completely unbothered. Square.

### Full Cup
Hold a mouthful of water. Everyone else does whatever they can to make you
laugh, no touching. (The sibling of Charlie's game — the body as the game
board.)

**Image:** a tight crop of one face, cheeks round, eyes streaming, a blurred
hand doing something just out of frame. Small square.

### The Vigil
Everyone stays in one room until only one person is awake. You can't leave the
room and you can't touch the sleepers. The last one up gets to decide one thing
about the next day, and everyone has to do it.

**Image:** a living room at 5am in blue-grey light, bodies over all the
furniture, one person sitting upright on the floor with a mug — the only figure
with any colour in her. Wide horizontal.

### The Lemon
A long con. Hide a lemon in someone's coat or bag. When they find it, they have
to hide it on someone else. It keeps travelling; you keep a log of where it's
been.

**Image:** two small pictures instead of one — a cutaway of a coat pocket with a
lemon in it, hanging on a hook by a door; and a little hand-drawn map with a
dotted line running between five houses. Small.

### Wrong Name
At a party where two people know you and nobody else does, introduce yourself
with a different name to every single person. The game ends the moment two
strangers compare notes in front of you.

**Image:** a party kitchen, her mid-handshake, and every other guest wearing a
small name tag with a different name written on it — all of them hers. Vertical.

### Museum Facts
In a gallery or a zoo, invent the wall text and say it to your friend at exactly
the volume strangers can hear. You win when you overhear a stranger repeating
your fact to someone else.

**Image:** a gallery wall holding one nearly blank painting, two friends
gesturing at it authoritatively, a stranger a few feet off leaning in to listen.
Wide horizontal.

### The Passenger
Nobody decides where you're going. At the start you make up a rule — the colour
of the next car, a coin, whatever's playing — and at every intersection the rule
picks left or right. The game ends when you arrive somewhere you would never
otherwise have gone.

**Image:** the windshield at night from the back seat: three silhouetted heads,
headlights, an unlit crossroads with no sign on it. Wide horizontal.

### Don't Touch the Floor
3am, someone's apartment, the floor is lava — but with the adult rule that the
game runs while you carry on with normal life. Getting a glass of water, finding
your phone, going to the bathroom, all of it off the ground.

**Image:** a kitchen at night from floor level — someone spread between the
counter and a chair, reaching for the tap, the whole bottom third of the picture
empty dark floor. Vertical.
